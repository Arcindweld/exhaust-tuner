# Job fixes, 2026-11-21: the reviewed proposals from the jobs QA round

The reviewer approved eight changes from docs/JOBS_QA_2026-11-20.md. Each one changes what the page shows. This file
records each one with before and after figures. Every change has a regression check in the new `web/tests/jobfix_ui.js`
(76 checks over 1440 and 400 px, about 12 s). jobs_ui.js is unchanged and still passes.

Base commit: ffdaf4e. Before figures come from that commit's page and after figures from this branch, in headless
Chromium at 1440 px. The scratch capture script reads the page the same way the tests do.

Hard rules kept:
- No other calculation result, default, input id, existing project-file key, URL hash or bridge contract changed.
- No pinned test number changed.
- No external resources were added.
- The new Quick input is `q-scope`. It is a new project key.

## 1. P1: the kind of muffler that ranks first

The catalogue's own data decides which vehicle a part is for. A new `partVeh(q)` returns:
- `bike` for the `moto` silencer type, or when the part's source sheet, model or words say motorcycle or bike. The 76
  Cone Engineering entries come from that maker's "Motorcycle Pre-Fabricated Mufflers" sheet.
- `car` when the entry names a car or truck. There are 20 of these: the Extended Exit race bullets ("side exit cars"),
  one Borla truck muffler and one Vibrant resonator.
- `null` for every other entry, which says nothing. No vehicle is guessed for these.

`findParts` now takes an optional `veh`. It drops parts for the other vehicle and keeps the parts that do not say. Within
the same loudness match, those unknown parts rank after the parts whose entry matches the vehicle. Quick ranks first by
its muffler-type preference, then by vehicle match, then by the finder's order.

The vehicle filter is applied in three places:
- **Quick.** A car is `car` and a bike is `bike`. Before, a bike was shown only the four built-in `moto` parts, and a car
  was shown everything else, including the Cone motorcycle cans.
- **Mufflers tab part cards.** The vehicle is the Sound tab engine's. The caption says which parts are left out and why.
- **Parts tab.** There is no ranking or suggestion list here: it is a browser filtered by brand, size and search. A part
  from a motorcycle sheet now carries a "motorcycle part" tag instead.

Inlet fit:
- A part is a fit only when its inlet is the main pipe's size (`partsFit`).
- When it is not, the heading reads "Mufflers with a … inlet" instead of "that fit". A note says the pipe needs a sleeve
  or a reducer.
- Before, a car with a 2.75 in pipe was told "Mufflers that fit a 2.5 in pipe".
- A bike now tries its own pipe size first, then the 2 in (51 mm) bike silencers.

Two further changes:
- A bug found by the full suite: a catalogue part sold in several inlet sizes lists them as an array (e.g. RaceMufflers'
  Oval Low Profile, 3 to 4.5 in). The inlet test then gave NaN, which passed every size, so the part was offered at any
  pipe size and its sheet line read "inlet NaN in". It now fits only at the sizes it is sold in, and the sheet names
  that size. These parts were also among the base's 67 Mustang matches, wrongly.
- The Mufflers tab at 2.5 in for the Mustang now lists the three Extended Exit bullets first, the car-said parts in the
  first loudness tier. Before, it listed the 2 in and 4 in long Stainless Headers bullets first.
- The Quick heading now gives both units: "Mufflers that fit a 2.5 in (64 mm) pipe". The old bike heading was "Bike
  silencers with a 51 mm inlet"; it is now "Silencers that fit a 2 in (51 mm) pipe".

Uncertain, for the reviewer: "rank it after ones that match" is applied inside each loudness tier (and, on Quick, inside
the muffler-type tier), not as the first key. Only race bullets say "car" at 2.5 in. As a first key they would put race
bullets ahead of the chambered Flowmasters on a moderate street Mustang. That one-line choice is in `findParts` and
`calcQuick`.

## 2. P4: the Headers tab's Smith's-rule row

The page keeps no link between the two engines. A preset or "Fill in every tab" writes the same displacement and cylinder
count to both tabs, so `sameEngineFH()` treats them as the same engine when:
- the displacements agree within 0.5 cc,
- the cylinder counts are equal,
- and the engine is a four-stroke.

When they are the same engine, the Headers row takes the 4-Stroke tab's:
- bore,
- stroke,
- valve count,
- and exhaust duration (its EVO, and its EVC or the same estimate).

The row then ends "…, all from the 4-Stroke tab's engine". When they are not the same engine, the label reads "Smith's
1950s open-pipe rule (estimated)". The row adds "a two-valve head is assumed: put the same engine on the 4-Stroke tab
(same displacement and cylinders) to use its bore, stroke and valves".

## 3. Tuning RPM differs between Headers and 4-Stroke

When the engine is the same and the rounded RPMs differ, each tab's key area shows a one-line note:
- Headers: "The 4-Stroke tab is tuned for 6,800 RPM; this tab for 8,000, so the primary lengths differ."
- 4-Stroke: "The Headers tab is tuned for 8,000 RPM; this tab for 6,800, so the primary lengths differ."

Each note gets a button through the page's existing pattern: `FIX_RULES`, the rule that gives a "do X first" note the
button that does X. The buttons are "Use the 4-Stroke tab's RPM here" and "Use the Headers tab's RPM here". They use a
new `copy` action, which copies the other field's value and fires its input event. Pressing either button clears both
notes.

## 4. P5: the Sound tab and the turbo

The note shows when all three hold:
- the Intake tab is boosted,
- the Sound tab's Induction is "None, naturally aspirated",
- and Induction was not set by hand in this session. "By hand" means a trusted change event on `s-ind`. Loading a project
  clears this flag.

The note's text and button depend on the Intake tab:
- **Turbo:** "The Intake tab is turbocharged; the induction here says naturally aspirated. A turbo damps the exhaust
  pulses." The button is "Set to turbocharged", a new `set` action. It sets the Sound tab to Single turbo.
- **Centrifugal supercharger:** the button is "Set to centrifugal supercharger".
- **Roots or twin-screw supercharger:** the Intake tab does not say which of the two, so the button is "Go to Induction"
  instead of a guess.

Nothing is changed on its own, so a saved project keeps its value.

"Fill in every tab" now works like this:
- Quick's own turbo still sets Single turbo.
- When Quick says Neither (or Supercharger) and the Intake tab has a turbo (or a centrifugal supercharger), the Sound tab
  follows the Intake tab.
- The message says so: "… (the Sound tab's induction follows the Intake tab: turbocharged)."
- Before, a Quick "Supercharger" gave the Sound tab "naturally aspirated". That still happens unless the Intake tab says
  centrifugal.

## 5. P6: the motorcycle build sheet

The pipe:
- A four-stroke bike's main pipe was the collector ID (or the header pipe ID) plus two 0.065 in walls. That is a tube
  nobody sells.
- It now snaps to the nearest size in `PIPE_OD_IN`, the stock list the car path picks from.
- The car rule is the smallest tube whose inside carries 98% of the flow-rule area. On the RS 660 that rule would give
  2.25 in, because a 2 in tube carries 97.8% of the collector's area. The brief asked for the nearest size, so it takes 2 in.
- The key figure shows "Main pipe 2 in, tube outside, 51 mm", as the car path does.
- The build text gives the exact figure beside it.

The resonator:
- The sheet now chooses between the two answers the page already computes: the J-pipe (quarter-wave side branch) and the
  Helmholtz can.
- On a bike it uses the J-pipe when it is no longer than 2 m. That is the page's own longest pipe a bike has room for,
  the drone check's limit.
- The can moves to Alternatives, with "Few motorcycles have room for a can that size".
- The steps and the hangers line now say J-pipe.
- No acoustics are new. The J-pipe is the `calcQW` the sheet already offered as its alternative.

## 6. P7: the 2-Stroke tab's header diameter

The tab already cites Jennings's *Two-Stroke Tuner's Handbook* (as summarized in "How Two-Stroke Expansion Chambers
Work") for "a header 10 to 15% larger in area than the port". That is a rule from a cited source, so it is now a hint
under "Header inside diameter" (`t-dh-jen`):

> Sizing the header: Jennings's *Two-Stroke Tuner's Handbook* (as summarized in "How Two-Stroke Expansion Chambers Work")
> makes it 10 to 15% larger in area than the exhaust port window, so for a window W wide and H high the inside diameter is
> √(4 × 1.10 × W × H ÷ π) to √(4 × 1.15 × W × H ÷ π). The default 1.625 in (41.3 mm) is only a starting figure: measure
> your port.

- The hint shows on the Jennings design only. On Blair's design the field is the port's own equivalent diameter, which
  already has its own hint.
- The default is unchanged.
- The rule needs the port window, and that design has no port inputs, so the hint gives the formula, not a figure.
- No cited source gives a rule from displacement alone.

## 7. P3: "What are you building?" on the Quick tab

The new select `q-scope` offers "Full system, headers back" (the default), "Cat-back: from the converters back" and
"Axle-back: mufflers and tailpipes only". Every sheet row, list item, note and alternative is tagged with its part of the
system: headers, converters, mid pipe, or axle back.

- **Full:** the sheet, list and order are as before.
- **Cat-back:**
  - Left out: the primary, collector and reducer-cone rows, the converters, and the head flanges, primary tube, merge
    collectors, cone, V-bands and oxygen sensor bungs on the list.
  - Kept: the main pipe, the crossover, the resonator, the mufflers and the tailpipes.
  - Added: "Clamps or flanges" to join the stock converter outlets.
  - Build order: it starts "Unbolt the stock system behind the catalytic converters…".
  - The note at the top reads: "Cat-back: kept from stock are the exhaust manifolds or headers, the front pipes and the
    catalytic converters with their oxygen sensors… Positions are still given from the head flange of the header this
    page would build: with the stock parts, measure from your own head flange."
  - "Your starting build" begins "What you build: a cat-back… the header and collector figures here are for reference
    only".
- **Axle-back:**
  - The sheet lists only the mufflers and the tailpipes.
  - The list has a "Tailpipe tube" in place of the main pipe tube, plus the clamps and the hangers.
  - The build order has four steps.
  - The note reads: "Axle-back: kept from stock is everything ahead of the rear axle…". On a bike it reads "a slip-on …
    the headers, any converter and the link pipe".
- **Two-strokes:** the sheet stays whole and says that the chamber is the whole tuned system.

The choice is saved in the project file under the new key `q-scope`. No existing key changed. A project without the key
(any saved before this change) loads as `full`. This uses the same pattern as the page's other legacy defaults in
`projApply0`.

Mustang GT, 180 in:

- Cat-back: 6 rows (main pipe, H-pipe, main pipe, Helmholtz resonator, mufflers, tailpipes) and 7 items.
- Axle-back: 2 rows and 4 items.
